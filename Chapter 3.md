# Astrellia
His resolve had never been the same as it was today. I had knownn him for almost three decades and always saw him _fix_ the mistakes that he started. I was honestly proud of him. But I couldn't shake the feeling that there was something deeper.

I had answered a help wanted ad asking for 'daily managerial tasks, including but not restricted to, keeping commitments, attending to financial matters, _et cetera_, etc."

I was fresh out of college, having this immense talent that I had gained throughout my school years. I had put it to good use in majoring for Information Sciences, something which had always been a special interest of mine. I would go into the libraries around San Fransico and reshelf _for them_. I honestly did not care that there was someone with that job, nor did I create more work for them. I was gifted in this way, and I inteneded to use it to the fullest. 

I was hit with immediate regret, almost the second I threw the cap over my shoulder. After searching fruitlessly on all the places one could get a job, I drew blanks. What pulled my eye towards Creator's listing was not only the incorrect style of his tagline (why use _et cetera_ and etc in the same sentence?), but the eccentricity of his writing, No one had written like this, except if they were dead. So a newspaper ad, actually written in this flowery manner? That, while I still to this day cannot put my finger on _why_, pulled me to it. So I wrote a letter to Creator's prompt. 

A few days later, I recieved a knock on my door. THe tall, caped man I would later come to know as Creator stood at my door as I opened it.

"I am present before you to offer my congratulations on obtaning employment with yours truly," he said, theatrically flourishing his cape as he invited himself in. He initally reminded me of Sherlock Holmes in his very antiquated mannerisms, even going as far as to hang his hat on the hook I had only used for the towels I used for myself before.

He surveyed the room with a practiced gaze. "I understand you live alone?"

Distrust, perhaps from the five years I spent being chased by men in college, seeped in my response.

"Why? Are you planning on… sleeping with me?"

He laughed, a hearty laugh that echoed throughout the barren apartment I lived in, barely enough for me to do as much. It was dingy to even me, used to the squalid conditions that came with being poor. I smiled, unsure if this strange man wanted to hurt me— the man who was attempting to be my employer, or already was. I still was unsure which of those it was.

But I didn't have to wonder any longer, as the next breath I took, he matched with a breathless, "You are the one I have chosen to help me wth my life. Will you?"

Against my better instincts, I said yes.
